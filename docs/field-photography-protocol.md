# Historical image provenance protocol — three-object alpha

The alpha uses historical photographs and other historical images retrieved from web-accessible collections. It does not use new field observations.

## Purpose

Images are treated as web provenance records, not decoration. Their evidentiary value comes from the source repository, catalog metadata, date/provenance information, depicted content, and relationship to a specific assertion.

## Priority order

For each of the three objects, search first for:

1. contemporaneous or near-contemporaneous photographs of presentation, installation, dedication, or an earlier location;
2. archival photographs that document object form, inscription, setting, or relocation at a known date;
3. digitized historical documents containing photographs or illustrations when repository provenance is clear;
4. later institutional images only when they add a distinct historical or identification function.

Do not build a generic image gallery. Every retained image needs a provenance or semantic purpose.

## Preferred repositories

Prefer institutional collections with stable catalog records: Library of Congress, National Archives, Smithsonian, National Park Service, government archives, national libraries, national museums, university special collections, and equivalent foreign public institutions.

Aggregator or reposted copies should lead back to the originating collection whenever possible. A visually identical image on two websites is not independent evidence when both derive from the same original photograph or negative.

## Image-source metadata

For each retained historical image record capture:

- source_id
- title / supplied caption
- holding institution or publisher
- collection
- catalog / item identifier
- creator / photographer when stated
- date or date range exactly as supplied
- source URL
- direct asset URL only when stable and useful
- retrieval date
- archived URL when available
- rights / reproduction statement
- original-language caption or description
- English translation when needed
- depicted object_entity_id
- source_family_id
- derived_from_source_ids when another web record is known to reproduce the same underlying image
- locator / page when the image is embedded in a digitized document

Do not silently convert an approximate catalog date into an exact date.

## Evidence semantics

A historical image can directly support what is visibly depicted at the cataloged time, subject to identification and dating uncertainty. Examples include the object being present at a particular documented setting, visible physical form, an inscription visible in the image, or evidence of a prior configuration.

A repository caption is a separate semantic layer from the pixels. If a catalog caption says an image depicts an event, person, date, donor, or provenance fact that is not independently visible, treat that statement as repository metadata rather than as a visual observation.

Historical photographs do not automatically verify authorship, donor identity, material origin, or object lineage. Those claims retain their predicate-specific documentary standards.

## Independence rule

Independence follows the underlying historical source, not the website. Two institutional catalog pages reproducing the same photograph are one evidentiary lineage unless one contributes genuinely independent metadata with its own documented basis.

## Three-object alpha priorities

### Cuban American Friendship Urn

Highest-value targets are images associated with the 1928 presentation, early Washington placement, later relocation history, or historical views where inscriptions/material configuration can be compared with documentary claims.

### José Gervasio Artigas

Prioritize historical images capable of distinguishing the Washington recast from the originating Uruguayan monument and documenting installation or early Washington context. Do not infer the foundry or recast date from appearance alone.

### José de San Martín

Prioritize historical images of the Washington monument before and after relocation and images of the Buenos Aires predecessor useful for documenting the copy relationship. Photography alone should not resolve the Daumas/Dumont attribution conflict.

## Publication gate

An image enters the alpha only when its institutional provenance, retrieval information, object linkage, source lineage, rights/reproduction information, and evidentiary purpose are recorded. If those cannot be established, the image remains outside the evidence set.
