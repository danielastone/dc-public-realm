# Uruguay archive search — José Gervasio Artigas monument, Washington

Status: remote reconnaissance and first press pass complete; underlying diplomatic files not yet examined.

## Research objective

Trace the Washington Artigas monument from Uruguayan-origin records rather than relying on later U.S. inventories. Keep distinct:

1. origin of the gift initiative;
2. fundraising / authorization;
3. identity and production lineage of the bronze;
4. casting / recasting location and responsible workshop;
5. shipment from Uruguay to the United States;
6. U.S. acceptance, pedestal/site work, and 1950 dedication.

A finding aid or later narrative is a research lead, not evidence for the underlying historical assertion.

## Primary Uruguayan repository

Archivo Histórico-Diplomático, Ministerio de Relaciones Exteriores / Instituto Artigas del Servicio Exterior.

The published classification guide identifies two overlapping record groups that cover the relevant period:

- Sub Fondo Cancillería, **Estados Unidos de América (1930–1956)**.
- Sub Fondo Legaciones y Embajadas, **Embajada de la República en Estados Unidos (1900–1990)**.

The archive states that inventories exist for nearly all documentary groups, but the detailed inventories are not currently exposed in the public web description. Therefore no box/folder numbers are inferred here.

### Questions for the archivist

1. Which files document the Uruguayan initiative to give an Artigas statue to the United States, reportedly begun around 1940?
2. Which records document fundraising by school children and any legislative appropriation?
3. Which records identify the exact bronze sent to Washington and its relationship to the San José monument?
4. Are there production, casting, foundry, workshop, invoice, contract, or transport records?
5. Which records document the reported April 16, 1947 shipment to the United States?
6. Which embassy/cancillería files cover U.S. acceptance in 1948, site/pedestal work in 1948–49, and the June 19, 1950 dedication?
7. Are there photographs, plans, inscriptions, speeches, programs, press clippings, or correspondence attached to those files?

## Remote evidence discovered during reconnaissance

### 1. Uruguayan diplomatic archive structure

The official archive classification confirms the relevant record groups and date coverage. This establishes where to search, but does not itself prove any monument assertion.

### 2. U.S. congressional acceptance — 1948

Senate Report 1439 (80th Congress, 2d session), accompanying S. 2591, states that the bill would authorize the Secretary of the Interior to accept the bronze Artigas statue **as a gift from the people of Uruguay** and erect it on federal property in Washington. This is a contemporaneous constitutive U.S. record for acceptance/donor wording, not for Uruguayan fabrication history.

Source: https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/SERIALSET-11207_00_00-292-1439-0000/pdf/SERIALSET-11207_00_00-292-1439-0000.pdf

### 3. U.S. State Department / FRUS lead

A 1950 Foreign Relations of the United States document recommends that the President personally accept the Artigas statue at the June 19, 1950 unveiling. Its editorial note states that the U.S. Government had accepted the gift in 1948 and that Secretary of State Dean Acheson participated in the June 19 ceremony as President Truman's personal representative. This is an independent U.S. diplomatic lead for the bilateral trail.

Source: https://history.state.gov/historicaldocuments/frus1950v02/d520

### 4. Commission of Fine Arts, 1948

Commission of Fine Arts minutes reproduce a November 1, 1948 letter to Uruguayan Minister José A. Mora stating that the Commission had considered the location of the Artigas statue on October 29, 1948, describing it as a gift accepted by congressional authority. The Commission selected Reservation 110 and recommended Harbeson, Hough, Livingston and Larson for the pedestal design.

This is a strong contemporaneous U.S. administrative record for site selection, but not a source for Uruguayan production lineage.

### 5. Contemporary Uruguayan illustrated report — *Mundo Uruguayo*

A digitized issue of *Mundo Uruguayo* contains the illustrated article **“Mundo Uruguayo en Washington: La estatua de nuestro héroe en la capital de Estados Unidos,” by Rivas Micoud**. The surviving page text reports the Washington inauguration and depicts Uruguayan ambassador Alberto Domínguez Cámpora unveiling the monument. It describes the statue as presented by the Uruguayan people, with special reference to Uruguayan schoolchildren, and records Alberto Lleras, Secretary General of the Organization of American States, speaking at the ceremony.

This is useful as a **contemporary Uruguayan witness to the dedication and donor narrative**. It does not, from the currently retrieved text, establish the foundry, recast date, or physical relationship to the San José bronze. Those remain archival questions.

Digital copy: https://digicoll.lib.berkeley.edu/record/293050/files/991054673619706532_v32_no1628.pdf

### 6. Pre-dedication Uruguayan press lead — 21 August 1949

Two independent indexes identify an item titled **“Monumento a Artigas en Washington” dated 21 August 1949**. The item appears in an index to the *Suplemento Dominical de El Día*. This is particularly valuable because it predates the June 1950 dedication and may preserve the Uruguayan account of production, financing, shipment, or planned installation before the later U.S. administrative histories were written.

The underlying article has **not yet been retrieved** and therefore is not evidence. The indexes are navigation evidence only.

Index sources:
- https://anaforas.fic.edu.uy/jspui/bitstream/123456789/5612/6/Musso%20-%20Indice%20general%20alfabetico%20del%20Suplemento%20Dominical%20de%20EL%20DIA.pdf
- https://studylib.es/doc/6680907/bolet%C3%ADn-hist%C3%B3rico-del-ejercito

### 7. NARA historical-image lead

The National Park Service publishes a historical photograph credited to the U.S. National Archives showing the Artigas statue with Uruguayan military personnel and a flag. The image is a useful locator for a NARA photographic record, but the NPS gallery page is not sufficient archival metadata to establish date/event without tracing the underlying NARA item.

Locator: https://www.nps.gov/media/photo/gallery-item.htm?gid=DC367B1B-3BB9-4D6F-BF4D-14D0A0C33DE3&id=2fdae5f7-daf4-40ff-b7c8-b0b7e98aa7f9

### 8. Later U.S. administrative-history claims to trace backward

A National Park Service administrative history states that the movement began in 1940, was completed in 1942, originated with Edgardo Ubaldo Genta, and that the statue was presented especially by Uruguayan schoolchildren. This overlaps heavily with later Smithsonian/NPS summaries and should be treated as a likely shared U.S. narrative until its footnotes/upstream records are traced.

Source: https://npshistory.com/publications/nace/adhi/chap3.htm

The current NPS object page repeats the Genta/schoolchildren narrative and says the statue was completed in 1948; Smithsonian instead records an early-1940s recast. The date disagreement is a reason to pursue primary Uruguayan production records rather than normalize these summaries.

NPS: https://www.nps.gov/places/000/general-jose-gervasio-artigas-memorial.htm
Smithsonian: https://www.si.edu/object/general-jose-gervasio-artigas-sculpture:siris_ari_325679

## What the press pass changes

The remote evidence now supports a cleaner division of labor:

- **1948 Congress / CFA / State:** strong U.S. evidence for legal acceptance, federal site planning, and diplomatic ceremony.
- **1950 Mundo Uruguayo:** contemporary Uruguayan evidence for the dedication and a people/schoolchildren donor narrative.
- **1949 El Día article:** highest-value remotely identified Uruguayan source still missing; likely the next best route into the pre-dedication narrative.
- **MRREE diplomatic files:** still the decisive route for production, shipment, formal Uruguayan transmission, and source genealogy.

The schoolchildren claim is therefore no longer merely a late Smithsonian/NPS claim: a contemporary Uruguayan publication appears to carry it too. That increases its historical provenance but does **not** yet establish whether the publication was independent reporting, ceremonial rhetoric, or derived from the same government publicity material.

## Epistemic priorities

### Highest-value retrieval

A contemporaneous Uruguayan diplomatic or administrative document that identifies:

- who initiated the gift;
- what physical bronze was selected or produced;
- who cast/recast it and where;
- its relationship to the San José monument;
- when and by whom it was shipped.

### Important distinction

Do not collapse **artistic lineage** into **physical production**. Existing sources can support Juan Luis Blanes / Juan Manuel Blanes / Dante Costa roles in the predecessor monument while leaving the Washington recast's actual foundry or fabricator unresolved.

### Dependency warning

Later NPS, Smithsonian, NCPC, and secondary narratives may share an upstream administrative history or James Goode. They must not be counted as independent corroboration merely because they appear on different websites.

Contemporary press is also not automatically independent. The *Mundo Uruguayo* article and the 1949 *El Día* article must be checked for diplomatic press-release language, common authorship, or reuse of official descriptions before they can count as independent corroboration.
