# Uruguay archive search — José Gervasio Artigas monument, Washington

Status: remote reconnaissance complete; underlying diplomatic files not yet examined.

## Research objective

Trace the Washington Artigas monument from Uruguayan-origin records rather than relying on later U.S. inventories. Keep distinct:

1. origin of the gift initiative;
2. fundraising / authorization;
3. identity and production lineage of the bronze;
4. casting / recasting location and responsible workshop;
5. shipment from Uruguay to the United States;
6. U.S. acceptance, pedestal/site work, and 1950 dedication.

A finding aid or later narrative is a research lead, not evidence for the underlying historical assertion.

Archivo Histórico-Diplomático, Ministerio de Relaciones Exteriores / Instituto Artigas del Servicio Exterior.

## Remote evidence discovered during reconnaissance

### 1. Uruguayan diplomatic archive structure

The official archive classification confirms the relevant record groups and date coverage. This establishes where to search, but does not itself prove any monument assertion.

### 2. U.S. State Department / FRUS lead

A 1950 Foreign Relations of the United States document recommends that the President personally accept the Artigas statue at the June 19, 1950 unveiling. Its editorial note states that the U.S. Government had accepted the gift in 1948 and that Secretary of State Dean Acheson participated in the June 19 ceremony as President Truman's personal representative. This is an independent U.S. diplomatic lead for the bilateral trail.

Source: https://history.state.gov/historicaldocuments/frus1950v02/d520

### 3. Commission of Fine Arts, 1948

Commission of Fine Arts minutes reproduce a November 1, 1948 letter to Uruguayan Minister José A. Mora stating that the Commission had considered the location of the Artigas statue on October 29, 1948, describing it as a gift of the Government of Uruguay accepted by congressional authority. The Commission selected Reservation 110 and recommended Harbeson, Hough, Livingston and Larson for the pedestal design.

This is a strong contemporaneous U.S. administrative record for site selection, but not a source for Uruguayan production lineage.

### 4. Contemporary Uruguayan press lead

A digitized issue of *Mundo Uruguayo* contains an illustrated article, “Mundo Uruguayo en Washington: La estatua de nuestro héroe en la capital de Estados Unidos,” reporting on the Washington inauguration and showing Ambassador Alberto Domínguez Cámpora unveiling the monument. This should be captured as a dated contemporary Uruguayan publication and checked for production/donor details rather than treated only as illustration.

### 5. Newspaper-index lead

A historical index records an item titled **“Monumento a Artigas en Washington” dated 21 August 1949**. The underlying article must be retrieved before it can be used as evidence.

### 6. U.S. administrative-history leads requiring source tracing

Later Smithsonian/NPS records contain unusually specific claims: the movement began in 1940; the work was completed by 1942; school children and the Uruguayan legislature financed it; wartime transport restrictions delayed shipment; General Genta shipped the bronze on April 16, 1947 without prior knowledge of the U.S. embassy. These claims are valuable search keys, not yet independent primary evidence. The Uruguay archive search should specifically test them.

## Epistemic priorities

### Highest-value retrieval

A contemporaneous Uruguayan diplomatic or administrative document that identifies:

- who initiated the gift;
- what physical bronze was selected or produced;
- who cast/recast it and where;
- its relationship to the San José monument;
- when and by whom it was shipped.

### Important distinction

Do not collapse **artistic lineage** into **physical production**. Existing sources can support Juan Luis Blanes / Juan Manuel Blanes / Dante Costa roles in the predecessor monument while leaving the Washington recast's actual foundry or fabricator unresolved.

### Dependency warning

Later NPS, Smithsonian, NCPC, and secondary narratives may share an upstream administrative history or James Goode. They must not be counted as independent corroboration merely because they appear on different websites.
