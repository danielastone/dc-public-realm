# Baseline source audit — source documents before claims

Date: 2026-09-29

## Rule

This audit starts with the underlying document and then asks which atomic claim it can support. It does not begin with the existing claim label and look for language that can be made to fit it.

Repository, publisher, and agency identity are not substitutes for claim genealogy. A later government or museum record can be useful evidence without being an independent historical root. Exact physical-object lineage claims require evidence identifying the same predecessor object; broad "copy" or "recast" language is not enough when the source points to a different object or location.

## Artigas

### Senate Report 1439 / S.2591

The 1948 U.S. Senate report supports the contemporaneous U.S. acceptance framing. Its legislative language describes the statue as a gift from the people of Uruguay. The same report also recounts an Uruguayan Embassy communication offering the statue as a gift from the Government of Uruguay, and describes schoolchildren and the Chamber of Deputies as funding participants.

Claim treatment:
- retain direct contemporaneous support for the U.S. acceptance/donor wording;
- do not collapse "people," "Government," and funding actors into one undifferentiated donor role;
- later NCPC and Smithsonian repetitions do not automatically create independent roots.

Source: https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/SERIALSET-11207_00_00-292-1439-0000/pdf/SERIALSET-11207_00_00-292-1439-0000.pdf

### Uruguay Resolution 732/004

The 2004 heritage resolution directly identifies the San José de Mayo Artigas monument and records the 1894 Juan Luis Blanes commission, the later Juan Manuel Blanes development of the boceto, Dante Costa's modeling/plaster/casting obligation, and the Florence bronze. Resultando VII explicitly includes Washington among foreign bronzes copying the San José monument.

Claim treatment:
- promote this source to direct primary support for the exact canonical relation A-0004: Washington Artigas -> copy/recast lineage -> San José de Mayo monument;
- retain it as later official heritage evidence, not contemporaneous 1890s evidence;
- its historical roots remain untraced unless the underlying commission/contract records are retrieved.

Source: https://www.impo.com.uy/bases/resoluciones/732-2004

### Smithsonian Artigas inventory

The Smithsonian inventory says the Washington sculpture is a recast of a Blanes Artigas sculpture cast in Florence in 1898, but it says that predecessor stands in Montevideo. The canonical object for A-0004 is the San José de Mayo monument.

Claim treatment:
- the record cannot remain direct support for that exact physical-object relation;
- preserve the mismatch as contradictory evidence rather than silently normalizing Montevideo to San José;
- the inventory lists NPS 1985, a 1991 Smithsonian project, James Goode 1974, and internal curatorial material among its references, so it is not presumed an independent root.

Source: https://www.si.edu/object/general-jose-gervasio-artigas-sculpture:siris_ari_325679

### MNAV Juan Luis Blanes record

The museum material supports Juan Luis Blanes's involvement in the San José Artigas project and his intention to supervise casting, but it does not itself establish the exact 14 November 1894 commissioning act.

Claim treatment:
- retain only limited corroboration for A-0007A;
- use Resolution 732/004 for the formal commission proposition until the underlying 1894 record is retrieved.

## San Martín

### 1924 congressional report / State Department transmission

The congressional material contains multiple embedded documentary layers: a presidential transmission, a Department of State letter, and proposed joint-resolution language. It contemporaneously establishes gift wording and proposed copy language, but the embedded copy text does not itself identify the canonical predecessor object by a Buenos Aires object identifier.

Claim treatment:
- retain contemporaneous support for A-0101;
- downgrade its A-0102 edge to supporting corroboration for general copy status rather than direct proof of the exact Buenos Aires physical-object relation;
- do not assume the embedded records are independent of one another.

Source: https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/SERIALSET-08253_00_00-041-0043-0000/pdf/SERIALSET-08253_00_00-041-0043-0000.pdf

### 43 Stat. 667, Public Resolution 31

The enacted joint resolution, approved 7 June 1924, says in its title that the statue was presented by the people of Argentina and authorizes erection of a copy described in the statutory text as "by Dumont."

Claim treatment:
- ingest the enacted law as its own source rather than treating the congressional report as a proxy for final law;
- use it as direct contemporaneous evidence for the legal donor wording;
- use the statutory "Dumont" wording as strong contemporaneous contradictory evidence against the canonical Daumas attribution;
- do not treat that art-historical wording as an independent root until its drafting ancestry is traced.

Source: https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/STATUTE-43/pdf/STATUTE-43-Pg667-2.pdf

### Argentine Daumas sources

The Instituto Nacional Sanmartiniano page on the 1862 Buenos Aires statue states that Louis-Joseph Daumas received the commission and explicitly cites nineteenth-century Buenos Aires municipal reports. A separate institute page identifies Washington (1925) among foreign copies of the Daumas Buenos Aires statue and cites Revista Legado / Archivo General de la Nación (2016).

Claim treatment:
- preserve both as later official-hosted research, not as independent primary archival roots;
- use the international-copy page as direct support for the exact A-0102 Buenos Aires-to-Washington lineage;
- record its explicit source inheritance and retrieve the cited AGN/Revista Legado material next;
- retain the Daumas/Dumont conflict as CONTESTED rather than selecting a national-source preference.

Sources:
- https://sanmartiniano.cultura.gob.ar/noticia/la-estatua-a-san-martin-en-la-plaza-de-marte-1862/
- https://sanmartiniano.cultura.gob.ar/noticia/monumento-a-san-martin-en-madrid/

### National Register San Martín nomination

The nomination identifies the Washington object as a copy of a Buenos Aires work and attributes that work to Augustin-Alexandre Dumont. Its historical bibliography is later secondary material, including James Goode.

Claim treatment:
- retain the copy statement as supporting corroboration;
- retain Dumont as material contradictory evidence;
- explicitly deny independent-root credit until the upstream source is identified.

Source: https://npgallery.nps.gov/GetAsset/5f4d13f8-f2c4-49af-849b-f45a9c3f8411

## Cuba

The baseline's principal historical provenance claims currently depend heavily on the 2007 National Register nomination and later NPS/NCPC summaries. The nomination itself points backward to earlier material including a 1929 Public Buildings/Public Parks annual report and contemporary 1928 material.

Claim treatment in this transaction:
- no historical Cuba assertion is upgraded from later synthesis;
- current custody and NRHP registration can remain direct administrative/constitutive claims;
- donor, presentation, material provenance, and design-lineage assertions stay as presently qualified until the cited 1928-1929 primary records and NARA RG 59 material are inspected;
- missing or inaccessible Cuban-side records remain an archival gap, not negative evidence.

## Transaction consequence

TX-20260929-002 implements only claims for which the underlying documents reviewed here justify a concrete edge correction. It deliberately leaves unresolved inheritance as UNKNOWN rather than manufacturing independence.

The principal expected epistemic consequences are:
- Artigas A-0004 becomes explicitly contested because Smithsonian's physical-predecessor wording conflicts with the San José relation directly stated by Uruguay's heritage resolution.
- San Martín A-0102 loses its shortcut to verification from generic contemporaneous copy language and is supported by a later source that explicitly identifies Washington as a copy of the Buenos Aires Daumas object.
- San Martín A-0106 remains contested, now with the enacted 1924 "Dumont" wording represented directly rather than only through later U.S. heritage synthesis.
- San Martín A-0101 gains the final enacted 1924 source without assuming it is independent of the earlier diplomatic/legislative chain.
