# Utility-Oriented Photography Framework

## Principle

The project should distinguish subject category from the practical function of a photograph.

A Metro elevator, hospital entrance, parking-garage entrance, and trailhead can all solve the same user problem: **where exactly do I go?** Conversely, two photographs of the same facility may serve completely different functions.

## Initial utility vocabulary

- `recognition` — identify the correct physical place or object
- `wayfinding` — locate a specific entrance, exit, path, transfer, or access point
- `arrival` — understand where to arrive by car, transit, bicycle, or on foot
- `accessibility` — document the accessible public route or entrance
- `engineering_documentation` — reveal structure, materials, condition, or asset context
- `cultural_identification` — identify a public artwork, monument, marker, or historic feature

## High-utility target families

### Tier 1 pilot candidates

1. Metro street elevators and accessible entrances
2. Public/customer restrooms where location or access is not obvious
3. Hospital main and emergency-department entrances
4. Complex transit entrances and transfer points

### Tier 2 candidates

- public parking-garage vehicle and pedestrian entrances
- trailheads and public trail access points
- ferry/water-taxi docks
- public libraries and recreation centers
- EV charging locations within complex lots or garages
- government public-service entrances

## Evidence rule

Utility attributes that can change — restroom availability, accessibility status, elevator status, entrance configuration, construction conditions — are dated observations rather than permanent facts.

The repository should prefer authoritative operator or agency sources for these attributes.

## Metro-specific opportunity

WMATA states that every Metrorail station has step-free access and street-to-mezzanine elevators, with signage identifying accessible elevator entrances. In 2026 Metro is also rolling out a revised wayfinding system that explicitly addresses entrances, exits, transfers, and elevator location.

This creates a useful repeat-photography opportunity: document the actual public-facing access point and, where relevant, changes in wayfinding rather than merely photographing station architecture.

## Restroom-specific opportunity

WMATA's customer-restroom inventory is dynamic. The agency's August 31, 2026 update reports 36 stations with customer restrooms available for use, of which 17 currently have at least one accessible customer restroom. Accessibility work is underway at the remaining stations in that inventory.

Photographs should therefore document location/recognition rather than imply that a restroom is open or accessible indefinitely. Current operating status belongs in a dated observation field and should be verified with the operator.

## Quality test

Before adding a utility photograph, ask:

> What concrete question does this image answer that a user could not answer as easily from the existing gallery?

If there is no clear answer, the image is probably low-value duplication.
