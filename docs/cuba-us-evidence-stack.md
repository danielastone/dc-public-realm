# Cuban American Friendship Urn — U.S. evidence stack

## Purpose

This stack reconstructs the U.S.-preserved evidentiary trail for the Cuban American Friendship Urn without treating better U.S. archival survival as proof that the U.S. account is inherently more reliable than unavailable Cuban records.

Three propositions remain separate throughout:

1. **Presentation / acceptance** — what Cuba presented, who presented and received it, and what the United States legally accepted.
2. **Object identity** — whether the object described in each record is the Washington urn.
3. **Material provenance** — whether the Washington urn was physically carved from material belonging to the Havana Maine Monument damaged in 1926.

Evidence establishing proposition 1 does not automatically establish proposition 3.

## Stack

### US-CU-01 — State Department diplomatic file — retrieval target

**Repository:** National Archives and Records Administration  
**Record group:** RG 59, General Records of the Department of State  
**Series:** 1910–1929 Central Decimal File  
**File:** `837.413M28` — USS Maine memorial in Cuba  
**Known item:** `837.413M28/16`, Despatch No. 172, Noble Brandon Judah to Secretary of State, April 10, 1928  
**Microfilm:** National Archives Microfilm Publication M488, roll 55

NARA states that file `837.413M28` concerns the USS *Maine* memorial in Cuba and identifies Despatch No. 172 as describing the 1928 ceremonies. The entire file is on M488, roll 55.

**Current epistemic state:** `RETRIEVAL_TARGET`. NARA's description establishes the file's existence, identity and relevance. It does **not** establish the detailed contents of the unexamined underlying file.

**Retrieval objective:** inspect the complete 1926–1928 run around `/16`, not only the single despatch. Capture incoming/outgoing correspondence, enclosures, translations, diplomatic notes, photographs, memoranda and references to Cuban-origin documents.

**Critical questions:**

- What exactly did Machado/Cuba present to Coolidge in January 1928?
- Does a contemporaneous record say the urn was carved from a fragment of the Havana Maine Monument?
- If so, who supplied that statement and on what basis?
- Does the file preserve an inscription, photograph, fabrication record or Cuban diplomatic note that independently ties the stone to the predecessor monument?
- How is the donor described: Republic/Government of Cuba, President Machado, citizens/people of Cuba, Havana, or another formulation?

**Source:** NARA, “Department of State Records Relating to the Destruction of the USS MAINE,” 2023.

### US-CU-02 — Commission of Fine Arts contemporaneous administrative record

The National Commission of Fine Arts annual report records that, during Coolidge's January 16, 1928 visit to Havana, the Republic of Cuba presented him a marble urn for erection in Washington. It further records that Lt. Col. U. S. Grant III, Director of Public Buildings and Public Parks, proposed a Rose Garden location and that the Commission adopted the proposal.

**Direct authority:** presentation as understood by the U.S. planning process; federal site-selection chronology.  
**Not direct authority:** fabrication/material chain in Cuba.

This source is particularly useful because it is close in time to the transfer and records the administrative consequence of the gift rather than retrospectively reconstructing the sculpture's history.

### US-CU-03 — Congressional acceptance / statutory trail

NCPC's memorial catalog and later NPS federal records identify congressional authorization in 1928 and cite **45 Stat. 713**. The exact public resolution text should be retrieved and ingested as the constitutive U.S. acceptance record before using it as direct evidence.

**Current epistemic state:** `LEGAL_CITATION_IDENTIFIED / PRIMARY_TEXT_PENDING`.

**Direct authority once retrieved:** legal acceptance, authorized federal action and statutory donor/object wording.  
**Not direct authority:** Cuban fabrication facts unless Congress expressly incorporates them and their upstream source is identified.

### US-CU-04 — Office of Public Buildings and Public Parks / Army Corps administrative record

The 2007 National Register documentation cites the **1929 Annual Report of the Office of Public Buildings and Public Parks**, p. 64, for physical/administrative information. A later NPS administrative history reports that the urn was made from a fragment of one of the Havana Maine Monument columns, weighed seven tons, and was installed in the West Potomac Park Rose Garden about December 15, 1928.

**Retrieval objective:** obtain the 1929 annual-report page itself and determine exactly which statements are contemporaneous administrative observations versus later NPS synthesis.

**Potential direct authority:** arrival, weight, installation, federal custody and perhaps contemporaneously recorded object description.  
**Material-provenance authority:** depends on the wording and provenance of the 1929 statement.

### US-CU-05 — National Register nomination, 2007

The NPS National Register documentation is the strongest currently accessible U.S. synthesis. It states that the urn was carved from a fragment of the Havana monument destroyed in the 1926 hurricane; separates the urn's material lineage from the derivation of its relief/composition from Felix Cabarrocas' Maine Monument; records the sculptor as unknown; and describes the 1928 presentation and congressional acceptance.

It also cites earlier records, including the 1929 Public Buildings/Public Parks annual report.

**Role:** `LATER_OFFICIAL_HERITAGE_SYNTHESIS_AND_SOURCE_MAP`.

Do not count this as independent corroboration of any underlying record it cites. Its highest epistemic value is often navigational: it tells us which earlier federal records to retrieve.

### US-CU-06 — National Park Service current object record

The current NPS object page repeats the material-provenance and diplomatic narrative and provides current administrative context.

**Direct authority:** current NPS identification/custody/site interpretation.  
**Supporting only:** 1926–1928 historical claims until their source genealogy is traced.

### US-CU-07 — National Capital Planning Commission memorial record

NCPC records the urn as authorized and dedicated in 1928, federally owned by NPS, and describes it as a gift from the Government of Cuba to the United States, presented by President Machado to President Coolidge.

**Direct authority:** current planning/memorial catalog fields and federal-land context.  
**Supporting only:** historical donor wording. The Government-of-Cuba wording conflicts with the National Register's citizens-of-Cuba formulation and should remain unresolved until contemporary acceptance/diplomatic records are examined.

### US-CU-08 — Later federal administrative continuity

NPS's 1985 *Sculpture in the Parks* records the urn as then “In Storage,” identifies the sculptor as unknown, describes it as sculpted from fragments of a Havana Maine memorial column, records presentation by the Republic of Cuba to Coolidge in January 1928, and cites the May 22, 1928 congressional authorization.

**Value:** establishes that the federal administrative narrative existed well before the 2007 National Register nomination and documents the object's custody state in 1985.  
**Dependency warning:** it may itself derive from earlier Public Buildings/Public Parks files; it is not automatically independent evidence of the 1928 material chain.

## Archival asymmetry rule

The U.S. stack documents what survives and is accessible on the U.S. side. It must not be presented as the complete bilateral record. Lack of a corresponding accessible Cuban record is `ARCHIVAL_GAP`, not `CONTRADICTS`, `NO_EVIDENCE`, or evidence that the U.S. formulation is historically superior.
