## San Martín — Washington copy lineage

The Instituto Nacional Sanmartiniano page explicitly says that copies of Louis-Joseph Daumas's Buenos Aires equestrian San Martín exist abroad and lists Washington, D.C. (1925). Crucially, it identifies its source as *Revista Legado*, Archivo General de la Nación, Publicación Digital Nº 3, August 2016. A newer Instituto page concerning the Quito replica independently describes the earlier page as based on Revista Legado Nº 3 by Nicolás Gutiérrez and links the PDF.

Decision: the Washington-copy assertion remains directly supported by the Instituto page, but the next provenance node is now specifically identified as Nicolás Gutiérrez, Revista Legado Nº 3 (AGN, August 2016), rather than an unspecified archival root. The magazine itself is still a later research publication, not a nineteenth- or early-twentieth-century primary record. Do not call this a primary root until its citations for Washington are inspected.

## Artigas — 1894 commission and 1896 Costa contract

The official Diario Oficial reproduction of Resolution 732/004 states two distinct historical propositions: on 14 November 1894 the San José commission resolved to entrust realization of the statue to Juan Luis Blanes; and on 25 July 1896 a contract obligated Dante Costa to model in clay, form in plaster, and have the figure cast in bronze, following six photographs of the relevant sketch.

Decision: these remain direct statements in a later official heritage/legal record. The wording proves that the 2004 state record was specific enough to distinguish the 1894 commission decision from the 1896 Costa contract. It does **not** prove that the 1894 minutes or 1896 contract have yet been inspected by this project. The root-source gap therefore becomes two named archival targets rather than a generic 'Artigas primary source' gap.

Source:
- https://impo.com.uy/diariooficial/2004/08/10/documentos.pdf

## Cuba — contemporary claim roots

The 2007 National Register nomination states that the urn was presented to Calvin Coolidge in Havana in January 1928, that Congress authorized acceptance on 22 May 1928, that its relief derived from Felix Cabarrocas's Maine Monument, and that its sculptor is unknown. Those are still later heritage assertions until their cited contemporary records are inspected.

A separate U.S. Department of Justice brief, relying on the National Park Service's *Sculpture in the Parks*, describes the Cuban Friendship Urn as presented by the president of Cuba to President Coolidge and accepted pursuant to a joint resolution of Congress. This is useful corroboration but is plainly derivative, not a new historical root.

Decision: do not promote the Cuba donor/material/design claims on the strength of another federal summary. The next useful primary target is the 22 May 1928 congressional acceptance instrument and the contemporary presentation record; the 1929 Public Buildings and Public Parks annual report remains a separate target for early federal custody/site history.
