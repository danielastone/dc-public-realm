# Root-source audit 3 — Cuba object evidence and early federal records

Date: 2026-09-29

## Scope

This pass tests the remaining Cuban Friendship Urn claims against evidence closer to the object and event, rather than adding another modern institutional summary.

## Object inscriptions as web-accessed primary evidence

The 2007 National Register nomination transcribes and describes two inscriptions physically attached to the urn. One inscription states that the urn was carved from a fragment of the marble column of the Havana Monument to the Victims of the *Maine*, whose column was toppled by the 20 October 1926 hurricane. A second inscription is attributed to Gerardo Machado and records the friendship purpose/presentation context.

For provenance purposes, the inscription itself is the evidentiary object; the NPS nomination is the web-access path and transcription witness. This is not a field observation by this project.

Decision:
- create a source record for the contemporaneous object inscription, with the NPS nomination as the retrieval/transcription path;
- use the material inscription as direct primary support for A-0205 (physical material provenance);
- use the Machado inscription only as corroboration for A-0203 (presentation by Machado), because an inscription can memorialize a presentation without proving the mechanics of the event;
- do not infer sculptor, financing, or private contributors from either inscription.

Web source: https://npgallery.nps.gov/GetAsset/a64ed8ed-b4b9-4b4f-bda8-17ef5726a6e7

## 1929 Office of Public Buildings and Public Parks annual report

The National Register nomination explicitly cites the 1929 Annual Report of the Office of Public Buildings and Public Parks, p. 64, for an early federal physical description. The nomination also states that the urn was installed in the Rose Garden at the southern end of West Potomac Park in 1928. A later NPS administrative history reproduces the early administrative description: acceptance authorized May 22, 1928; presented to Coolidge by Gerardo Machado; made from a fragment of a column of the Havana *Maine* monument; marble; seven tons; installed in the Rose Garden about December 15, 1928.

Decision:
- the 1929 annual report is now a named primary retrieval target rather than an unspecified historical source;
- do not ingest the later NPS administrative history as an independent root for those propositions;
- retrieve the annual report itself before using it to upgrade installation, material, or presentation claims.

## House Report 1346 and Congressional Record

The April 1928 Monthly Catalog identifies House Report 1346, 70th Congress, 1st session, accompanying H.J.Res. 265, as a one-page report on the monumental urn gift from the Republic of Cuba. The Congressional Record records House consideration of the measure and substitution of S.J.Res. 125. These documents can expose the legislative chain behind Public Resolution 49, but they are not independent roots for the enacted legal donor wording.

Decision:
- retrieve H. Rept. 1346 as a legislative-history source;
- model it as upstream/downstream within the same legislative claim family once inspected;
- do not count the Congressional Record, committee report, and enacted statute as three independent corroborations merely because they are separate documents.

## Historical-photo search

A targeted Library of Congress/National Archives web search did not surface a clearly identified 1928 photograph of the urn in this pass. LOC does surface later HABS documentation for West Potomac Park and the Tidal Basin that helps with site history, but those records are not substitutes for a contemporaneous object photograph.

Decision: keep the historical-photo hook open. A collaborator/archive request should target December 1928 Rose Garden installation imagery and the January 1928 Havana presentation, with repository/call-number capture required before any image becomes evidence.

## Epistemic consequence

This pass strengthens material provenance through a contemporaneous inscription without pretending that a web transcription is a new institutional root. It also narrows the remaining Cuba archive work to specific documents and image events. No claim is upgraded solely because later federal publications repeat the same history.
