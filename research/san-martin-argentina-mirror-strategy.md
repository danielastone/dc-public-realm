# San Martín — Argentina mirror provenance strategy

## Objective

Reconstruct the Argentine evidentiary lineage for the Washington San Martín monument independently of the U.S. lineage. Do not treat later Argentine institutional consensus as proof of what Argentine actors knew or asserted in 1921–1925.

The mirror is intentionally symmetrical:

| U.S. lineage | Argentine lineage |
|---|---|
| State Department / RG 59 | Foreign Ministry / diplomatic correspondence |
| Commission of Fine Arts / RG 66 | Club del Progreso + monument commission / technical-production records |
| Congress / presidential transmission | Argentine executive / legislative records where relevant |
| LOC / NARA photographs and documents | AGN / Biblioteca Nacional / institutional archives |
| U.S. newspapers | La Nación, La Prensa and other contemporary Argentine press |
| NPS / NRHP / HALS synthesis | Instituto Nacional Sanmartiniano / AGN / later Argentine synthesis |

The two national graphs should meet only after each has been reconstructed on its own terms.

## Working source genealogy

Current Argentine-side hypothesis:

`Club del Progreso proposal (1921)`
→ `Subcomisión Pro Monumento / Carlos A. Aldao, Pablo Ricchieri, Adolfo Pueyrredón, Franklin Rawson`
→ `national subscription + participating institutions`
→ `technical production / Arsenal Esteban de Luca`
→ `Argentine diplomatic transmission / Honorio Pueyrredón and Foreign Ministry`
→ `U.S. State Department / Congress`
→ `Washington dedication (1925)`

This is a research hypothesis, not a proven chain. Every arrow needs documentary support.

## Highest-priority Argentine repositories

### 1. Archivo General de la Nación (AGN)

Search for records of the monument commission, government correspondence, photographs, shipping, diplomatic ceremony, and any retained Club del Progreso material. AGN is also important because later Instituto Nacional Sanmartiniano material cites *Revista Legado*, an AGN publication, for the international Daumas-copy tradition.

Priority terms:

- `Monumento San Martín Washington`
- `Club del Progreso monumento San Martín`
- `Carlos A. Aldao Washington`
- `Honorio Pueyrredón monumento Washington`
- `Estanislao Zeballos San Martín Washington`
- `American Legion monumento San Martín 1924`
- `Arsenal Esteban de Luca monumento San Martín`

### 2. Archivo Histórico de la Cancillería Argentina

This is the Argentine mirror of NARA RG 59 and should be treated as the highest-value unresolved documentary target.

Seek diplomatic correspondence concerning:

- offer and acceptance of the gift;
- wording used to identify the sculptor and original work;
- negotiations over site and authorization;
- correspondence with the U.S. Department of State;
- shipping and customs arrangements;
- instructions to Ambassador Honorio Pueyrredón;
- dedication planning and official speeches.

Critical epistemic question: **Does Argentine diplomatic correspondence sent to Washington identify the source statue as a work of Daumas?** If yes, and U.S. records subsequently say Dumont, the divergence can be localized inside the U.S. documentary chain. If Argentine diplomatic records themselves say Dumont, the divergence moves upstream.

### 3. Club del Progreso archive / institutional records

The 1947 Instituto Nacional Sanmartiniano article identifies the Club del Progreso as the initiating institution and describes a July 28, 1921 unanimous approval, a 10,000-peso opening subscription, and a four-member subcommission. These are retrospective claims until the underlying Club minutes, circulars, subscription records, or correspondence are located.

Targets:

- 1921 general assembly minutes;
- Comisión Directiva minutes;
- Subcomisión Pro Monumento correspondence;
- subscription ledgers;
- contracts / fabrication records;
- correspondence with Argentine ministries and U.S. officials;
- 1923 exhibition documentation;
- 1924 shipping documentation.

### 4. Biblioteca Nacional Mariano Moreno

Use the Biblioteca Nacional as the Argentine analogue to LOC for printed and newspaper evidence. Preserve issue, page, column, supplied title, date, and digitization/catalog provenance.

Priority newspapers:

- **La Nación**
- **La Prensa**

Search 1921–1925, with concentrated passes around:

- 28 July 1921 — Club del Progreso approval;
- 1923 — reported completion/exhibition of monument components;
- 1 July 1924 — shipment aboard *American Legion*;
- October 1925 — dedication coverage;
- 28–30 October 1925 — speeches, cables, editorials and ceremony reports.

A later bibliographic index preserved by the Universidad de Buenos Aires identifies *La Prensa* issue 19,444 of 29 October 1925 as carrying Honorio Pueyrredón's Washington speech and a José León Suárez conference tied to the inauguration. Treat that index as a locator, not as the underlying newspaper evidence.

### 5. Instituto Nacional Sanmartiniano

Treat the Instituto primarily as a **finding aid and later synthesis layer**, not automatically as an independent root.

Two especially useful records are already identified:

- the 1947 Francisco Ojam article, republished by the Instituto, describing the initiative and realization of the Washington monument;
- the Instituto's monument inventory, which identifies Washington as a Louis-Joseph Daumas copy and states that its source is the Archivo del Instituto Nacional Sanmartiniano.

The 1947 article is unusually valuable because it names actors, institutions, materials, dates, shipping arrangements and intermediary events that can be tested against earlier records. But it was written more than two decades after the event, so it does not supersede contemporaneous evidence.

## Contemporary press independence rules

Do not count newspaper mastheads as independent evidence by default.

For each article classify:

- `ORIGINAL_REPORTING`
- `OFFICIAL_TEXT_REPRINT`
- `DIPLOMATIC_RELEASE_DERIVED`
- `WIRE_AP`
- `WIRE_OTHER`
- `EDITORIAL`
- `UNKNOWN_ORIGIN`

The 1947 retrospective says Associated Press attended the 1924 Buenos Aires shipping ceremony. That creates a concrete dependency risk for international reports of the event.

La Nación and La Prensa also participated materially in the monument project: the 1947 account says they financed the bas-reliefs. Their contemporary reporting is therefore evidence from institutions that were also project participants. Encode that relationship rather than treating their coverage as detached observation.

## Historical images

Search Argentine repositories for images of:

1. the Buenos Aires Daumas original before 1921;
2. the Washington reproduction while in Argentina;
3. the 1923 display of bas-reliefs / cornerstone at Club del Progreso;
4. the 1 July 1924 shipping ceremony;
5. fabrication or assembly at Arsenal Esteban de Luca;
6. dedication-related Argentine imagery or transmitted photographs.

For every image distinguish:

`pixels` → observable visual evidence

`catalog metadata` → repository assertion

`caption printed with original image` → contemporaneous textual evidence

`later caption` → later interpretive metadata

Never merge these layers into one source assertion.

## Daumas / Dumont conflict — mirrored test

The central attribution problem should now be tested from both directions.

### Argentine chain

`Buenos Aires original`
→ `Daumas authorship evidence`
→ `1921–24 reproduction commission records`
→ `Argentine diplomatic description sent to U.S.`

### U.S. chain

`Argentine diplomatic description received`
→ `State Department correspondence`
→ `Congressional language: “by Dumont”`
→ `later U.S. heritage/catalog records`

Do not resolve the conflict from modern institutional repetition. Locate the earliest occurrence of each name in each documentary lineage.

## Current web findings to preserve as leads

### 1947 Instituto Nacional Sanmartiniano retrospective

The Instituto republishes Francisco Ojam's 1947 account. It states that the initiative began among Club del Progreso members in 1921; identifies Aldao, Ricchieri, Pueyrredón and Rawson as the subcommission; describes national subscription support; says La Prensa and La Nación funded the bas-reliefs; identifies Argentine materials; says Arsenal Esteban de Luca technicians advised the commission; reports a 1923 exhibition; and records shipment aboard *American Legion* on 1 July 1924.

This is a dense **lead generator**, not a substitute for the underlying 1921–25 records.

### Instituto monument inventory

The Instituto's published monument record identifies the Washington work as by Louis-Joseph Daumas, gives inauguration dates of 28 October 1925 and 6 October 1976, identifies Club del Progreso and others as donors, and names the Archivo del Instituto Nacional Sanmartiniano as its source. This creates a specific archival target behind the modern record.

### 1926 Argentine presidential message

President Marcelo T. de Alvear's 1926 message to Congress refers to the U.S. President and people honoring San Martín at the inauguration of the Washington statue. This is useful as contemporaneous Argentine executive confirmation that the event had entered official state reporting, but it does not by itself establish authorship or donor mechanics.

### Argentine newspaper locators

A later UBA bibliographic index points to *La Prensa*, 29 October 1925, issue 19,444, for Honorio Pueyrredón's Washington speech and José León Suárez's conference on the inauguration. Retrieve the actual issue before using either as evidence.

## Acquisition sequence

1. **Find Argentine diplomatic correspondence first.** This is the decisive mirror to RG 59 and the best chance of localizing Daumas/Dumont.
2. **Recover Club del Progreso primary records.** Establish initiation, donor mechanics, commission membership, fabrication and wording used for authorship.
3. **Retrieve 1925 La Nación and La Prensa issues.** Classify article ancestry before counting corroboration.
4. **Search AGN/BNMM historical images.** Tie every image to a catalog record and source lineage.
5. **Only then reconcile with U.S. records.** Compare the two national graphs assertion by assertion.

## Publication rule

The public object page should eventually expose national provenance lanes rather than flattening them:

- **Argentina-origin evidence**
- **United States receiving/administrative evidence**
- **Contemporary press evidence**
- **Historical image evidence**
- **Later institutional synthesis**

A disagreement between the Argentine and U.S. lanes is a result to publish, not a defect to smooth away.
