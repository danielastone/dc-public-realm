# José de San Martín — archival reconstruction pass 1

Research date: 2026-09-28

Purpose: reconstruct the Washington monument from primary and archival records before relying on later inventory summaries. This file is a research layer, not a canonical assertion file. Claims move into canonical data only after their source lineage and claim-specific authority are encoded.

## 1. Congressional authorization — 1924

**Source:** Congressional Record, House debate and resolution, 1924, GovInfo.

URL: https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/GPO-CRECB-1924-pt10-v65/pdf/GPO-CRECB-1924-pt10-v65-8-2.pdf

The resolution authorized the Chief of Engineers to permit erection on federal public grounds in Washington of a **copy of the statue of Gen. San Martin, by Dumont**, with the site and pedestal subject to approval and no expense to the United States.

### Epistemic use

- Direct/constitutive for U.S. authorization and the wording Congress adopted in 1924.
- Direct for the fact that the authorized object was described by Congress as a copy.
- Direct for the fact that the enacted/considered U.S. text used the attribution “by Dumont.”
- **Not independently decisive for art-historical authorship.** The resolution may reproduce information supplied during the gift/authorization process. It therefore documents the U.S. government's contemporaneous attribution rather than resolving the Daumas/Dumont conflict.

Provenance root: U.S. Congress / legislative record.

## 2. Dedication and diplomatic transfer — October 28, 1925

**Source:** Calvin Coolidge address at the dedication, preserved in the Everett Sanders Papers, Library of Congress Manuscript Division; printed by the Government Printing Office.

LOC item: https://www.loc.gov/item/mss38893_07/

LOC resource: https://www.loc.gov/resource/amrlm.mss38893_07/?st=pdf

Catalog date: 1925-10-28.

The LOC catalog identifies the document as Coolidge's address at the dedication of a monument to General José de San Martín given by Argentina to the United States. In the address, Coolidge responds to the Argentine ambassador's presentation of the monument on behalf of Argentina.

### Epistemic use

- Contemporaneous primary evidence for the dedication event and date.
- Strong direct evidence that the monument was being presented from Argentina to the United States at the ceremony.
- Strong evidence for the diplomatic semantics attached to the object at transfer.
- Does not establish sculptural authorship or the fabrication history merely because those matters might appear in ceremonial rhetoric.

Provenance root: presidential address / Everett Sanders Papers / LOC Manuscript Division.

## 3. Dedication program — NARA photographic record

**Source:** National Archives digitized dedication pamphlet/image, October 28, 1925.

URL: https://s3.amazonaws.com/NARAprodstorage/lz/mopix/111/h/production/pdf/111-H-1170.pdf

The digitized item identifies the unveiling of the monument to General José de San Martín, presented to the people of the United States by the people of Argentina, at Judiciary Square, Fifth and E Streets NW, October 28, 1925 at 3:00 p.m.

### Epistemic use

- Contemporaneous event artifact for date, scheduled time, location and presentation wording.
- Separate archival root from the later NPS/NRHP synthesis.
- Before counting as independent corroboration of donor semantics, determine the creator/provenance of the program: if produced by the Argentine Embassy or copied from the same diplomatic source underlying another record, dependency should be encoded rather than assumed independent.

Provenance root: NARA-held/reproduced historical record; exact record-series provenance still to be resolved.

## 4. Original site — Judiciary Square

**Source:** HABS, Judiciary Square, HABS No. DC-690, Library of Congress.

URL: https://tile.loc.gov/storage-services/master/pnp/habshaer/dc/dc0700/dc0799/data/dc0799data.pdf

The HABS chronology records the San Martín statue as erected in the center of Judiciary Square in 1925.

### Epistemic use

- Later federal heritage synthesis for location chronology.
- Useful as a pointer to underlying reservation/park records and historical photographs.
- Should not be counted as independent of those underlying records if HABS cites them.

Provenance root: HABS/LOC, with upstream sources to be traced.

## 5. Current memorial history and relocation

**Source:** HALS, General José de San Martín Memorial, HALS DC-68, Library of Congress.

URL: https://www.loc.gov/pictures/collection/hh/item/dc1246/

LOC catalog metadata records 1924 initial construction and subsequent work in 1970, 1976 and 1985. The associated documentation identifies the memorial as the first sculpture gift to Washington from a foreign nation and records congressional approval in 1924.

**Source:** HABS, Virginia Avenue, Reservation No. 106, Library of Congress.

URL: https://tile.loc.gov/storage-services/master/pnp/habshaer/dc/dc0800/dc0821/data/dc0821data.pdf

The Virginia Avenue history states that the equestrian statue was erected in Judiciary Square in 1925 and moved to Reservation 106 in 1976.

### Epistemic use

- Strong later federal synthesis for the spatial chronology.
- Not yet an independent root for the 1976 relocation because HALS/HABS may draw from NPS administrative records also underlying the National Register nomination.
- The next task is to identify the actual relocation/design/project file.

## 6. NARA record groups that can move the reconstruction upstream

### RG 42 — Office of Public Buildings and Public Parks of the National Capital

NARA guide: https://www.archives.gov/research/guide-fed-records/groups/042.html

Potential value: federal public-ground administration, monument/site records, plans and photographs. Search for San Martín, Judiciary Square, Reservation 106, monument relocation, and associated Chief of Engineers/public buildings files.

### RG 59 — General Records of the Department of State

NARA Record Group Explorer: https://www.archives.gov/findingaid/stat/discovery/59

Potential value: Argentine diplomatic correspondence, gift transmission, embassy communications, ceremonial arrangements and State Department handling.

### RG 66 — Commission of Fine Arts

NARA guide identifies Central Files, 1920–1986 (NAID 559488). CFA's statutory/advisory function covers location and design of statues and monuments in Washington.

Guide: https://www.archives.gov/research/african-americans/record-groups/rg-066-fine-arts

Potential value: site selection, pedestal/design review, artist attribution as understood during review, correspondence, photographs and relocation review.

### RG 328 — National Capital Planning Commission

NARA identifies RG 328 among civilian record groups available for research in Washington.

Research page: https://www.archives.gov/dc/research/civilian-records

Potential value: later relocation/site planning and Reservation 106 context.

### Independent NARA research lead

NARA's bibliography specifically identifies James F. Vivian, “Splendid Isolation: Argentina, the United States, and the San Martin Monument in Washington, 1921–1925,” as using **RG 59 and RG 66**.

URL: https://www.archives.gov/research/alic/periodicals/nara-citations/dc-government.html

This is not evidence for the object by itself. It is a high-value finding aid showing which archival record groups prior scholarship found productive.

## 7. Authorship conflict — preserve, do not solve by vote

Current source structure contains a genuine conflict:

- The 1924 U.S. congressional text calls the authorized copy a statue “by Dumont.”
- Later U.S. NPS/Smithsonian records repeat a Dumont attribution.
- Argentine official sources identify Louis-Joseph Daumas as author of the Buenos Aires original and describe Washington as a copy of that work.

The new archival material does **not** justify resolving this conflict yet. The congressional text strengthens the conclusion that “Dumont” was a contemporaneous U.S. governmental attribution, but it does not establish that the attribution was independently researched or correct.

### Required next evidence

Search RG 59 and RG 66 for the correspondence or submission that supplied the artist name to Congress/CFA. If “Dumont” can be traced to a specific Argentine diplomatic document, fabrication record, inscription, or independent expert review, its evidentiary status changes. If it merely propagates through U.S. records from one initial statement, the apparent multi-source consensus collapses to one lineage.

## 8. Newspaper layer

Search contemporary Washington and national newspapers around:

- 1924-06-07 authorization;
- shipment/arrival of the monument in 1924–1925;
- 1925-10-28 dedication;
- removal for Metro construction around 1970;
- 1976 relocation/rededication.

Newspapers should be modeled as event witnesses and reception evidence. A newspaper statement about sculptor, donor, or fabrication does not count as independent corroboration until its likely upstream source is assessed. Wire-service duplication counts as one lineage, not many newspaper sources.

## 10. Canonical-data gate

This pass establishes strong new primary/archival anchors for the 1925 dedication and original location, but it does not automatically change computed statuses. Before adding each source to `sources.json` / `assertion-evidence.json`:

1. identify the exact archival item or record;
2. classify what the source directly establishes;
3. identify likely upstream dependence;
4. encode `dependency_status` conservatively;
5. add only to assertions for which its authority fit is direct or clearly supporting;
6. never turn multiple descendants of the same archival root into independent corroboration.

The target is not more VERIFIED assertions. The target is a more accurate evidence graph.
