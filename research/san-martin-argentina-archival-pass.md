# San Martín — Argentina archival reconstruction

Status: web-first archival discovery pass. No field observations.

## Research question

Where did the sculptor attribution and other provenance facts transmitted from Argentina to the United States originate, and at what point did the Daumas/Dumont divergence enter the documentary chain?

## Confirmed Argentine archival targets

### Archivo Histórico de Cancillería — Embajada en Washington, Estados Unidos I (27.A)

The official guide describes this section as covering 1869–1946 in 156 boxes, paper, open access, with an inventory by conservation unit. This is the closest Argentine mirror of the U.S. State Department correspondence lane for the 1921–1925 gift.

Official guide: https://www.cancilleria.gob.ar/es/institucional/patrimonio/archivo-historico-de-cancilleria/atencion-usuarios/guia-de-fondos

Official inventory page: https://www.cancilleria.gob.ar/es/institucional/patrimonio/archivo-historico-de-cancilleria/atencion-usuarios/inventarios

The inventory page exposes an Excel inventory specifically titled `Embajada en Washington, Estados Unidos I`. Retrieval of the workbook contents remains open; the existence of the inventory is confirmed, but no box/folder number is asserted here until the workbook is actually inspected.

### Memoria del Ministerio de Relaciones Exteriores y Culto, 1924–1925

The Archivo Histórico publishes the ministry's 1924–1925 annual memory online. It is a contemporaneous government synthesis and a navigation source for the Washington mission and ministry activity. It should not be treated as independent from the underlying ministry correspondence when both concern the same transaction.

Record: https://cancilleria.gob.ar/userfiles/ut/memoriamrec_1924-1925_optimizado.pdf

### Compilaciones temáticas de la Dirección de Archivo (37.1)

The official guide states that this open 53-box collection includes a thematic grouping on José de San Martín. It contains mainly published material but in some cases original archival documents from ministry offices. Therefore it is a discovery target, not presumptively a primary source.

Record: https://www.cancilleria.gob.ar/es/institucional/patrimonio/archivo-historico-de-cancilleria/atencion-usuarios/guia-de-fondos/371

### Archivo access route

The Archivo states that its holdings run from 1810 to the present and that research-room access is available by appointment. Its user service can provide inventories; the official inventory page specifically says missing inventories may be requested from the archive.

Archive: https://www.cancilleria.gob.ar/es/institucional/patrimonio/archivo-historico-de-cancilleria

User access: https://www.cancilleria.gob.ar/es/institucional/patrimonio/archivo-historico-de-cancilleria/atencion-usuarios

## Current Argentine source layers

### Layer A — contemporaneous government records

Highest priority. Search 27.A and related ministry records for 1921–1925 correspondence involving:

- monumento / estatua / General José de San Martín;
- Club del Progreso;
- Carlos A. Aldao;
- Adolfo Pueyrredón / Honorio Pueyrredón;
- Estanislao S. Zeballos;
- American Legion / Munson Line;
- Judiciary Square;
- escultor / autor / Daumas / Dumont;
- pedestal, bajorrelieves, Arsenal Esteban de Luca;
- acceptance, shipment, customs, installation, dedication.

For each hit preserve sender, recipient, date, file/box locator, exact attribution wording, and attachments/enclosures. The enclosure is potentially more probative than the covering diplomatic letter.

### Layer B — organizational provenance

The Instituto Nacional Sanmartiniano republishes Francisco Ojam's 1947 account. It says the initiative began among Club del Progreso members in 1921; names Carlos A. Aldao and participating organizations; describes Argentine materials and Arsenal Esteban de Luca technical involvement; says parts were exhibited at Club del Progreso in 1923; and dates shipment aboard `American Legion` to 1 July 1924.

Source: https://sanmartiniano.cultura.gob.ar/noticia/el-monumento-al-general-san-martin-en-washington-la-iniciativa-y-su-realizacion/

This is a later narrative and therefore a lead generator. Its assertions should be traced to Club del Progreso minutes, committee records, correspondence, invoices, technical records, or contemporary press before being promoted.

### Layer C — later Argentine institutional attribution

Argentina's national cultural heritage page identifies the Buenos Aires equestrian group as the work of Louis Joseph Daumas. The Instituto Nacional Sanmartiniano's Washington monument catalog material likewise identifies the Washington work as Daumas and cites its own archive.

Heritage record: https://www.argentina.gob.ar/escultura-jose-de-san-martin-y-los-ejercitos-de-la-independencia

This establishes a current Argentine institutional attribution tradition. It does not establish what wording was transmitted to Washington in 1923–1924.

### Layer D — newspapers

Contemporary Argentine press is a separate lane only after ancestry is classified. `La Prensa` and `La Nación` helped finance the Washington monument's bas-reliefs according to the 1947 account, so their reporting is not automatically disinterested independent corroboration of the project narrative.

For each article classify:

- ORIGINAL_REPORTING
- OFFICIAL_TEXT_REPRINT
- DIPLOMATIC_RELEASE_DERIVED
- WIRE_COPY
- EDITORIAL_OR_PARTICIPANT
- UNKNOWN_ORIGIN

Associated Press repetition across newspapers is one source family, not multiple corroborators.

## Attribution divergence test

Maintain two chronological lanes without reconciling them editorially.

### Argentina lane

1. Buenos Aires predecessor attribution.
2. Club del Progreso / monument committee attribution.
3. fabrication / Arsenal / technical attribution.
4. Argentine Foreign Ministry outgoing record.
5. Argentine Embassy Washington record.
6. Argentine dedication materials and press.
7. later Instituto / heritage catalog tradition.

### United States lane

1. Argentine communication received by State.
2. Secretary of State correspondence.
3. presidential transmittal.
4. congressional authorization (`Dumont`).
5. CFA documentation.
6. dedication documentation.
7. later NPS/HABS/HALS catalog tradition.

The decisive object is the earliest document in either lane that actually names the sculptor. Record the name exactly as written. Do not normalize `Daumas` and `Dumont` into one entity until the documentary relationship is demonstrated.

## Epistemic rules

- A modern Argentine government page saying `Daumas` establishes the modern institutional attribution, not the 1924 transmission.
- A 1924 diplomatic letter quoting an enclosure and the enclosure itself are dependent evidence; retain both but trace the claim to the enclosure.
- An Argentine newspaper copying an embassy release is dependent on the diplomatic lane.
- A newspaper reporter independently describing a visible signature or contemporary monument documentation can be independent for that observation.
- Multiple web reproductions of the same historical document remain one underlying source.
- Archive finding aids prove the existence and archival organization of holdings, not the substantive monument facts expected to be inside them.
- Negative web search results never establish absence from the paper archive.
